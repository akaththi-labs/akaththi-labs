The problem

  • Every device and tool keeps its own data. Nothing shows the whole farm in one place.
  • Records live on clipboards and in chats, so one batch cannot be compared with the next.
  • Know-how lives in a few people's heads and leaves when they do.
  • Nobody can trace a pack on the shelf back to the batch, the inputs and the climate it grew in.

It starts at the chamber

  1. Sense. Sensor nodes read temperature, humidity, CO2 and light.
  2. Decide. The controller compares readings with the settings for the crop and its stage.
  3. Act. Relays switch cooling, humidifier, fans and lights.
  4. Record. Every reading and action is logged for dashboards and later analysis.

What is inside

  • Sensor and actuator nodes running firmware on ESP32 microcontrollers.
  • An edge controller on Raspberry Pi hardware, next to the chamber.
  • Live dashboards for the grower.
  • Open standards where they exist, such as MQTT with Sparkplug B.

Farm to market

  • building Chamber control and live dashboards.
  • planned Procedures built in, so the know-how stays with the farm.
  • planned Crop cycles and batches, tracked from start to harvest.
  • planned Raw material and inventory records.
  • planned Harvest, packing and sales records, so produce can be traced to the market.
  • planned Models for climate tuning, yield forecasts and contamination detection. They need many logged cycles first.

Until a grow cycle has run end to end on our farm, we are not publishing performance numbers.
